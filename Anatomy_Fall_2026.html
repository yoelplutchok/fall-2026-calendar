Where the points actually are

Anatomy does not publish the Biochemistry-style per-module score rows. A full Canvas sweep of assignments, quizzes and assignment groups returns a single graded item: the Pre-lab Quiz. The 60% written and 30% practical weights are therefore denominators over the whole semester, not eight separate exam percentages.

ComponentWhat the sources sayWeight
Module written examsOrientation: total Anatomy questions correct across the written exams, divided by the total number of Anatomy written questions. Deck says 7 exams; calendar and modules carry 8.60%
Lab practicalsOrientation: total correct across the practicals. Deck says 3 lab exams, then "across the 2 exams" on the next line. Calendar carries three: Sep 15, Oct 6, Nov 23.30%
Interactive sessionsPoll Everywhere with a location restriction — responses only count from inside the classroom. Confirmed on the orientation deck; Poll Everywhere also appears in the Canvas course navigation.5%
Lab participationPre-lab Quiz, assigned worksheets, 100% attendance, bone boxes returned on time, clean dissection table, lab-instructor input. Course director's decision is final.5%
  • Confidence on the 60/30/5/5 split: high. Final V1.0 syllabus and the orientation deck agree, and the calendar page already carries the same table.
  • Confidence on pooling: medium-high for written, medium for practicals. The orientation deck states pooling plainly, but contradicts its own exam counts. No Canvas gradebook rows exist yet to confirm or refute the mechanism. If pooling holds, one weak exam dilutes rather than isolates — which is the opposite of Biochemistry's per-module score rows.
  • Indicative load, not points: interactive-session counts on the Group B calendar are roughly Foundations I 2 · Foundations II 4 · MSK I 4–5 · MSK II 5 · CP I 4 · CP II 1 · GI 7 · Renal 2. That is a teaching-time proxy only. It is not a published point table.

The method, and why this course is not Biochemistry

Previous-year guidance frames Anatomy as top-down and spatial against Biochemistry's bottom-up memorisation. The lab and practical need their own system entirely.

  • Top-down first. Skim Netter or walk the lab for a mental map of regions before memorising attachments. DHahn: Exam 1 thinking is a "2D map"; later exams need a "3D globe" of relationships. In 2026 that shift still happens, but across MSK and thorax rather than across two standalone papers.
  • Videos are denser than the slides. Some faculty leave explanations only in the recording. Keep a running file of timestamps the lecturer flags as exam material.
  • Questions early, not as a final check. Order that keeps being recommended: objective sheet, then Gray's/BRS, then TA reviews (older years first if short on time, then newest), then Poll Everywhere decks, then missed questions only.
  • Origins, insertions, innervations, exceptions. Handmade artery diagrams and anastomoses on slides are high yield. Exceptions to rules are examined more often than the rules.
  • Embryology rides with the region. It is not a separate course grade. Each block's embryo lectures are examined on that block's written.

Lab and practical — their own system

  • Written and lab are one course. Dissection time is also written study if the table is used to quiz relationships, not only to cut.
  • Do not save lab for the week of the practical. DHahn: go 1–2 times a day near the exam; first pass every structure on the list, then partner quiz, then timed passes.
  • Wall of Shame / image stations. Study the released images in black and white — colour will not be there. Image-occlusion Anki is the recommended tool once the deck exists. Prior-year decks help for skill, not for identical pins.
  • Practical rules that cost points: do not touch specimens or tags unless told; pencils and clipboards are provided; you may edit earlier answers until time is called; swaps require a form and a documented emergency.
  • Group B clock times: Anatomy labs Tue/Mon 3–5 PM, Thu 1–3 PM, with the Sep 23 and Oct 22 exceptions flagged in the weekly plan.
  • More than five minutes late = absent. The 5% participation grade is easy to keep and easy to lose.
