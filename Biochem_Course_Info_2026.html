Exam mechanics

Format

Multiple choice in board format, delivered through ExamSoft. Each exam is shared across all the courses in that module, and Biochemistry contributes a block of questions to it rather than sitting its own paper.

Five options is the norm in faculty-written questions, with four appearing in some sets. "None of the above" is effectively absent from this course.

What is supplied

The metabolic map is provided in every exam. Both the syllabus and the orientation deck state this, which is why fluency in navigating it matters more than reproducing it. The blank copy is FullSubwayMap222.pdf, and there is an interactive version for practice.

The NBOME Reference Adult Laboratory Values sheet is also supplied. Know where each value sits on it.

Timing

No admission more than 15 minutes after the scheduled start. Exams run two hours except GI, which runs 9:30 AM to 12 PM.

Start times vary between 9:30 and 11 AM across the eight, so each one needs checking rather than assuming.

Missing one

A makeup is only offered if the absence is formally excused by the Dean of Student Affairs, and the course director decides its format. Hospitalisation and a death in the family are the examples the syllabus gives.

Without a formal excuse, a makeup is capped at 90% of the raw score if it is allowed at all.

On memorising numbers. Across 449 faculty-written questions measured from archived material, 5.8% turn on a specific number, 2.2% ask for a calculation and 3.8% require reading a figure. Central-concept language outnumbers minor-detail language by more than twenty to one. The numbers worth committing are the ones the posted topic lists name explicitly — the ETC energy yields, the fasting glucose range, the ATP yield from one glucose — and one or two of those per exam is the realistic expectation.

The 8% — the cheapest marks in the course, and the easiest to lose

  • Polling responses during interactive sessions are graded for participation, not for correctness. Answering wrong costs nothing. Not being in the room costs the point. Roughly 21 sessions carry one to two points each, and arriving late means missing the first questions of the set.
  • The polling app is named two different ways by the course's own documents. The Fall 2026 syllabus says Poll Everywhere for the 8%; the Fall 2026 orientation deck says iClicker. Install both, log in to each before Aug 4, and settle it at the first session. Poll Everywhere is the campus-wide attendance tool and has a mandatory authentication step, so do that setup in advance either way.
  • Attendance at the interactive sessions is required by college policy. All sessions are in person and there is no remote option. Absence from a session does not remove responsibility for the material covered in it.
  • The Vitamins team-based learning session on Thu Nov 12 counts toward this component and carries a peer contribution form. Read the TBL outline before the session rather than in it.
  • Supplemental sessions are optional and carry no marks — but the five sitting in the week before an exam are exam reviews, and those are worth attending on content grounds.

The 2% — how the quizzes actually behave

  • 22 graded quizzes, 163 quiz points, 11 deadlines, all closing at 11:59 PM. They are graded for correct answers. Full deadline table on the calendar page; open them from the Canvas quiz list.
  • Two documents describe two different systems, and Canvas is the one the deadlines run on. The orientation deck describes a Monday 8 AM to Sunday 11:59 PM window with three attempts. Canvas unlocks each quiz roughly two weeks ahead at 8:00 AM and closes it at 11:59 PM on the day of the module exam it belongs to, with attempt limits of three, six, twenty-five or unlimited depending on the quiz. The syllabus defers to Canvas — it says only "due dates on Canvas". Read the due date and the attempt limit on each individual quiz.
  • Nine of the eleven deadlines fall on or after the exam the quiz belongs to, and two fall well after: the Globular and Fibrous Proteins quiz closes Nov 1 on content examined Oct 6 and Oct 19, and the final three close Dec 13, four days after the Renal exam. Work each quiz in the week its lectures are taught and treat the deadline as a backstop.
  • Attempt limits are not uniform within a week. The three quizzes closing Aug 17 have unlimited, six and six attempts respectively. The MSK 1 and MSK 2 quizzes have three each, which makes them a one-shot review rather than a drill.
  • Around thirty ungraded practice quizzes exist alongside the graded set, with unlimited attempts, plus a not-for-credit duplicate of most self-assessments. Those cost nothing and can be repeated indefinitely.
  • Four quizzes in the gradebook carry 2025 dates and are not on this year's schedule: a Week 2 Pedigree Inheritance quiz, a Week 2 quiz marked F25, a Week 7 Regulation of Gene Expression quiz, and a duplicate Week 15 Vitamins and Iron quiz.

How the course is taught

Flipped, with two session types

Interactive sessions assume the week's videos have been watched. First ten to fifteen minutes of question and answer, then thirty-five to forty minutes of polling questions. Attendance counts.

Supplemental sessions are a mini review of that week's interactive session plus student questions. In the week before an exam they become exam reviews. Optional, ungraded.

What is expected before class

All pre-class work done: the assigned lecture videos watched, the relevant textbook chapters read, any required quiz submitted. Class time is for application — peer teaching, team-based learning, case-based learning and problem-solving.

Six to eight polling questions are embedded through each lecture video as well.

What the clinical vignettes ask of you

Analyse the information given, independently identify the genetic or biochemical basis of the disorder, then suggest a differential diagnosis and appropriate management using what the lectures and sessions covered.

All fourteen vignette subjects are examined; several recur across five to seven distinct years of archived material.

The stated philosophy

From the orientation deck, unedited: "Your patients will not have a biochemistry problem — they will have a medical problem." And: "Most questions need thinking, not just memorizing."

The learning outcomes are written the same way — illustrate the reactions, explain the integration of the pathways, identify how disruption leads to disease.

Absence, tardiness and makeup policy

  • Attendance is required for the interactive sessions and for every scheduled exam. Absence for any reason does not relieve responsibility for the material covered, and submission of the weekly Canvas quizzes is required to receive credit for them.
  • Permission to be excused from a required component is not routinely granted. Requests go through the Office of Student Affairs on the excused absence request form.
  • Exams: no admission more than 15 minutes after the scheduled start. A makeup requires a formal excuse from the Dean of Student Affairs; its format is the course director's decision. Without a formal excuse, any makeup is capped at 90% of the raw score.
  • Repeated tardiness is treated as a professionalism issue under the AOA core competencies and can carry disciplinary consequences.
  • Official closures affecting this course: Labor Day Sep 7, Yom Kippur Sep 21, and the Thanksgiving recess Nov 26–27. Half days ending at 12 PM fall on Sep 11, Sep 25 and Oct 2. Last day of class Dec 4; last day of finals Dec 11. Source is the registrar's TouroCOM academic calendar, which outranks the syllabi on closures.
  • If Zoom is used for a live session, cameras are expected on unless otherwise approved.

Four things where the course's own documents disagree or a gap exists. None is guesswork about policy — each is a specific question with a named person to ask.
